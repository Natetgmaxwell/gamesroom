# Canon Correction — 50/200h hour rule is Hall, not Waldinger

*Captured 2026-08-13 in the Games Room counter-trend brainstorm. For gbrain dream-cycle to ingest.*

## The error
Multiple brain pages cite "Waldinger" as the source of the 50/200h hour friendship rule. Waldinger's actual claim (Harvard Study of Adult Development) is that **the quality of relationships at age 50 is the strongest predictor of long-term health and happiness**. He did NOT originate the hours curve.

## The real source
**Jeffrey A. Hall, PhD** — University of Kansas, Department of Communication Studies. Published *"How Many Hours Does It Take to Make a Friend?"* in the *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*, 2019 (Vol. 36, Issue 8). Methodology: a 1998 survey of ~3,000 adults, re-analyzed in 2018. Findings:
- 50 hours = casual friend
- 90 hours = friend
- 200+ hours = close / best friend

Also from Hall:
- *"Friendship Formation Naturally Produces Conformity"* (2018)
- *"The Role of Communication in Personal Relationships"* is his broader area

## Waldinger's actual contribution (what to write instead)
- Robert Waldinger & Marc Schulz, *The Good Life*, 2023 (Simon & Schuster).
- The Harvard Study of Adult Development began 1938, still running — oldest longitudinal study on human flourishing.
- Core finding: **social connection quality at age 50 is the strongest correlate of physical and mental health in later life**, more than cholesterol, IQ, income, or social class.
- The 12-minute TED talk (2015): https://www.ted.com/talks/robert_waldinger_the_good_life_lessons_from_the_longest_study_on_happiness
