## 1. The hook

**Plans the night. Gets out of the way.**

Seven words. The locked hero (A+C directive, 2026-08-13). It names the product
(the app plans nights) and the refusal (it doesn't run them), and it carries the
whole counter-trend thesis without naming the trend. It teaches: a non-tech
reader hears what Games Room does. The sub-headline adds the cultural claim:

**Real tables. Real friends. No feed.**

The pairing is locked by user directive combining Option A (the product claim)
and Option C (the cultural claim): A teaches, C argues. Together they land as
"this is the product AND this is why your life is better for it." Two opens in
one story, no feed on either. [Source: NARRATIVE_HEADLINE_DIRECTIVE.md, 2026-08-13]

Alternates, kept for A/B by surface:

- "Real tables > real feed." (the cultural claim alone). The default hero for
  social posts, where the canvas is too small to teach. [Source:
  NARRATIVE_HEADLINE_DIRECTIVE.md]
- "Your friend group, with a memory." (the arc claim). The warm face of the
  counter-trend. Strong for the ledger/season story.
- "Your games night, counted." (the established landing voice). Kept on the
  website hero until the V0.55 deploy lifts this spine in. [Source:
  V0.43_GAMES_ROOM_WEBSITE_SPEC.md]

Carry-down per surface (locked): website hero leads Pairing 1 (H1 teaches);
in-app first-run leads the sub-headline and closes screen 3 with the H1;
social leads the H1 as topic-sentence and the sub-headline as punchline.
[Source: NARRATIVE_HEADLINE_DIRECTIVE.md]

## 2. The one-line thesis

**Games Room plans the night so you can live it. It brings the people to the
table, then gets out of the way.**

Twenty-one words. The anti-feed banner. It says what the app does (plans), what
it refuses to do (run), and where the value lives: the table, not the screen.
[Source: originals/2026-08-13-games-room-v053-vision-memo]

## 3. The problem

The host opens the group chat. "Who's in Friday?" Two replies, one maybe, then
the thread scrolls on. The night dies there: coordination is the whole job and
the thread is bad at it.

Feeds taught a generation to collect friends and keep none of them in a room.
The group has no memory, no arc, no season. Just isolated evenings that never
quite became a thing. [Source: concepts/games-room-market-context]

## 4. The promise

Three moments, three different people.

The host: three taps and the night is on. Table set, RSVPs in, nobody chased.
[Source: originals/2026-07-07-games-room-mvp-scope-1c6aaa]

The member: walks in, finds the chair card with their name, sits. No "is this
taken," no scan for where they fit.

The quiet member: reads the ledger before the game. Knows who is ahead and what
happened last time, so they walk in already in the story. [Source:
concepts/2026-07-27-games-room-profile-aware-social-design]

The night runs itself. The app is nowhere near the table.

## 5. The three personas

**Host.** Does the highest-cost work and wants to be present, not run the ring.
Wants the night to happen without costing the night. Hero line: "Plans the
night. Gets out of the way." [Source:
concepts/2026-07-27-games-room-profile-aware-social-design]

**Member (the invitee).** Wants to show up and belong without homework. Fears
the flake and the dead night. Wants the seat held and the night guaranteed.
Hero line: "Your seat's claimed. Just walk in." [Source:
COUNTER_TREND_BRIEF_OUTCOMES.md]

**Quiet member.** Carries the highest social cost. Wants to be in the story
without performing it. The ledger is their surface: they read it before the
game and join the room already oriented. Hero line: "Knows the story before the
door." [Source: concepts/2026-07-27-games-room-profile-aware-social-design]

## 6. The story arc

Ninety days, one group, no wipes.

Week 1: the chair card. The first RSVP that actually held.

Week 4: someone brought chips, unprompted, because the night had become a thing.

Week 8: the season-end award. The loser took the Drowning trophy and laughed.

Week 12: a friend-of-friend showed up, and did not feel new.

That is the arc the ledger carries and the feed cannot: nights compound into a
season, a season into a running joke, the joke into belonging. The shift toward
real friendship takes roughly ninety to two hundred hours together, which is
exactly what a recurring weekly table compounds. [Source: Jeffrey Hall, "How
Many Hours Does It Take to Make a Friend?", *Journal of Social and Personal
Relationships*, 2019; originals/2026-08-13-games-room-friendship-substrate-spec]

## 7. The anti-pillar

What we never say: "connect with friends," "make new friends," "play with
people online." No follows, no reach, no feed. The people are already yours.
[Source: originals/2026-08-13-games-room-v053-vision-memo]

## 9. The message triangle

Same spine, three openings.

- **(a) Hero / first-time visitor.** Lead with the hook, then the sub-headline,
  then the promise moments. "Plans the night. Gets out of the way. Real tables.
  Real friends. No feed. Three taps and the night is on."
- **(b) Onboarding step 1 in-app.** Lead with the action and the payoff. "Set
  the night. The room remembers it." The host's three-tap job, made concrete.
- **(c) Instagram / X.** The cultural claim with no setup. "Real tables > real
  feed." Or the arc beat: "Week 12: a friend-of-friend showed up and didn't feel
  new."
